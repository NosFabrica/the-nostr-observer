The Nostr Observer · pre-flight

Before the Model Call

This service prints one personal newspaper a day from a reader's web of trust. Writing it costs about a dollar fifty and several minutes of somebody else's relay. Everything on this page runs first, for free — and every state here is a different sentence to tell the reader.

4 trust checks · 12 outcomes 2 storage checks · 5 outcomes 2 of them stop the run everything else is an instrument

00 · What the reader needs on Nostr

Three lists and a scoring service

The paper is built from a ranked read of the last 24 hours. The ranking is the product: without it you get the same firehose everybody else gets. Three lists the reader publishes, and one large set of events somebody else publishes about them, decide whether that ranking is possible at all.

kind 10002
Relay list (NIP-65). Where the reader publishes. The outbox model, used here for what it is for: their profile, their follows and their own notes live wherever they said they would.
kind 10040
Trust provider list (NIP-85). Who the reader has appointed to work out their web of trust. It names a service pubkey, a dimension, and the relay that service publishes to.
kind 30382
Trust score cards (NIP-85). Published by that service — one per person it scored, from that reader's point of view. A big set: about 149,000 cards for one reader in the measurement below.
kind 10063
Blossom server list. Where the finished paper is uploaded. Needed to publish an edition, never to make one.
the search relay
A NIP-50 relay that imports those cards and extends search with two tokens: one naming a viewer, one asking for that viewer's ranking. It also answers NIP-45 COUNT, which is how the checks below ask “how many, and how many should there be?”
The failure everything here is built around

A ranked query naming a viewer whose scores the relay does not have does not fail. It quietly ranks the query for nobody in particular. The result looks exactly like a working feed and is not one: on a measured window, that anonymous ranking was 209 of 400 posts from a single spam account.

There is no error to catch and no empty result to notice. So the checks have to happen up front, before a request is made that is expensive to get wrong.

01 · The gate

Two chains, one of which can stop you

The trust chain runs first and decides whether there is a paper to print. Nothing is generated for a reader who has not cleared it — no fallback feed built from follows, no “here is roughly what it would look like”. They are told which single thing is missing instead.

The storage chain runs alongside it and never stops anything. A reader with no Blossom server can read today's paper; they just cannot publish it. It is asked here, at the start, because the alternative is telling somebody they have nowhere to put their paper after they have paid for one.

02 · How the chain behaves

Four properties, none of them obvious

Rule 1

The first missing thing wins

Every check below the break reports waiting, never a second failure. A column of red crosses says four things are wrong when one is, and sends the reader off to fix three that are fine.

Rule 2

Counting the cards is not enough

A relay can hold a scoring service's cards and not yet be ranking with them — the import and the indexing are separate. Only running a ranked search and comparing it to the unranked one catches that.

Rule 3

“Your own posts are behind” is an aside

The reader's own notes are not what the ranking needs. Folded into the chain, it tells somebody whose web of trust is perfectly healthy that their search is broken — and the fix for it is nothing at all.

Rule 4

No answer is not zero

“We did not get a count” and “the count is zero” are different sentences with different next steps. NIP-45 is optional; a missing denominator draws nothing rather than estimating.

03 · The four checks

What each one asks a relay

The two reads and the two searches run at once. The four COUNTs run one at a time: run concurrently against this store, they hang — it sends an AUTH challenge before answering a COUNT even though its NIP-11 document says authentication is not required, and four handshakes racing on one socket is not something a client can fix from the outside.

Check 4 runs no matter what checks 1–3 said. It is two cheap searches, and the only thing that can see a relay holding cards it is not yet ranking with.

Check The question Asked as What a bad answer looks like
1 · your relay list Do we know where you publish? read your kind 10002 no event, or nothing in it on wss://
2 · your scoring service Have you appointed someone to rank for you? read your kind 10040 no event, or no usable rank entry in it
3 · your trust scores Have that service's cards reached the relay we search? COUNT kind 30382 by that service — here, and on the service's own relay zero here: the relay answered, and holds none
4 · ranking your feed Does a ranked read actually come back? the same 24-hour search twice — once naming you, once naming nobody the unranked one returns rows and yours returns none
— · your own posts Is your own history caught up? (an aside) COUNT your kind 1 here, and on your own relay nothing — it never blocks anything
Why check 4 has a 24-hour window on it

That search returns 12 events immediately with a since of 24 hours, and times out returning nothing without one. Both sides then come back empty, which reads as a quiet day rather than a broken web of trust — so the check passes every time while testing nothing. It shipped that way once.

06 · Somewhere to publish

The second chain, asked at the start on purpose

A finished edition is uploaded to the reader's own Blossom servers and announced on their relays, so it lives in their archive rather than ours. That needs a kind 10063 and a signature at upload time — and neither has anything to do with whether a paper can be written.

It used to be checked at publish time, which is after an edition exists and has been paid for: the reader found out they had nowhere to put their paper at the one moment the answer was most expensive.

07 · Past the gate, still before the call

What the day's reading has to survive

  1. Read the window — one subscription per section

    Notes, pictures, long-form, live streams, classifieds and the rest each get their own REQ, sent together, alongside one more: the same 24 hours read without the reader's ranking. Sharing one subscription meant sorting the answers out by kind afterwards, which works right up until two filters share a kind — and two do, since the unranked control is kind 1 exactly like the notes section. Merged, its results were filed as news.

    Every ranked filter also carries a minimum score. That is not the same as a limit: it turns “the top 400” from a target back into a ceiling, so a thin web of trust or a quiet day stops scraping downward for people nobody vouched for.

  2. Stop if the window is empty

    Zero notes ends the run. A thin paper is the right response to a quiet day; nothing at all is not a thin paper. This is the second and last thing that can stop a run before the model is called.

  3. Compare against the unranked read — an alarm, not a gate

    How much of the ranked window also appears in the anonymous one. Near zero is the whole product stated as a number. Near 100% means the two reads have been merged by mistake, announced in the progress list rather than discovered in an edition three weeks later. Nothing stops on it.

  4. Shortlist the pictures

    Images are hotlinked — never fetched, resized, re-hosted or inlined. Nothing in this step can fail on somebody else's server, because nothing in this step touches one.

  5. Trim the day to fit

    About 62,000 tokens, divided as an even share per section rather than spent in order — otherwise the notes section ate the budget and everything below it printed nothing. What was kept and what was cut is reported before the call, not after.

  6. Only now: write the paper

    One streaming call. The model writes the whole front page — markup and layout — from the day's reading and a fixed prompt the reader never sees or influences. Roughly a dollar fifty, and everything above exists so that it is a dollar fifty spent on a paper somebody can actually be shown.

08 · Outside the chain

Four guards that are not checks

That is a secret key

stops

the pasted string starts with nsec1

  • ✕what you pastedrejected before any filter is built

That is a SECRET key. Paste your npub or hex public key instead.

Worth its own guard, because the obvious decode does not protect you: a general bech32-to-hex helper happily turns an nsec into 32 bytes that look exactly like a pubkey. Without this line, somebody pasting their private key into the front door would have it put into a relay filter and sent over the wire.

09 · The other side of the call

What deliberately is not checked here

Three checks run after the model writes, and belong there. The model writes the whole document rather than filling a template, and safety is enforced on what comes back rather than by constraining what it can say.

The page is stripped: no script, no remote loads, and no link to the open web survives — only addresses pointing back at the source events. It is then opened in a browser, and if it does not render as a readable page it is written once more, and after that reprinted in the house layout. Finally it is fact-checked against the day's events: every quotation has to be found in a note that was actually read. A page that quotes something nobody said is not published, though the reader who paid for it still gets to read it.

That division is the shape of the whole thing. Everything on this page is about whether the call is worth making. Everything after it is about whether the answer is worth keeping.

The numbers here are readings taken against one relay and one reader in August 2026 — 149,266 cards from one scoring service, 35,084 notes in a day, 302 accounts network-wide with a web of trust published for them. They are measurements, not constants. Re-measure before trusting any of them.
