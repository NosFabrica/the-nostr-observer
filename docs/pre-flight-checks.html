The Nostr Observer · pre-flight

Before the Model Call

A front page costs about a dollar fifty and several minutes of somebody else's relay. Everything below runs first, in order, for free — and every one of these states is a different sentence to tell the reader.

Readiness.kt · ReadinessProbe.kt · Press.kt lens chain: 4 links, 12 verdicts storage chain: 2 links, 5 verdicts gate: verdict.ranks

01 · The gate

Two chains, one of which can stop you

Press.gather is everything up to but not including the model call. It runs the readiness chain first — the pre-flight the whole design turns on, because the failure it catches is silent. Every filter this service sends carries observer:<pk> sort:rank, and that token resolving to nothing does not error: it quietly becomes the anonymous ranking. On a measured window that was 209 of 400 posts from one spam account. Finding that out after the model call is finding it late.

So the chain is the gate. A reader who has not cleared it gets Refused(NO_LENS) and the name of the one unmet link — never a paper built some other way.

02 · How the chain behaves

Four properties, none of them obvious

Rule 1

The first unmet link wins

Every link below the break reports WAITING, never a second failure. A column of red crosses says four things are wrong when one is, and sends the reader off to fix three that are fine.

Rule 2

The ranked probe is not redundant

Cards can be present and not yet projected — the trust projection is per service, derived by a reconcile that runs at relay startup. Only asking the authed and the anonymous read the same question catches that.

Rule 3

Your own posts are an aside

Ranking is complete without them. Folded into the chain, it tells a reader whose lens is perfectly healthy that their search is broken — and the fix for it is nothing at all.

Rule 4

Null is not zero

“We have not looked” and “this relay holds none” are different sentences with different next steps. NIP-45 COUNT is optional; a null denominator draws nothing rather than estimating.

03 · The lens chain

Four questions, asked of the relay

ReadinessProbe.gather talks to the network and leaves every fact it cannot establish null; Readiness.assess is pure and decides. Links 1, 2 and 4 run concurrently — link 4 regardless of the rest, because it is the only thing that can see a service whose cards are stored but not yet projected. The four COUNTs run one at a time: run concurrently, --check went from three seconds to hanging until killed, because this store sends an AUTH challenge before answering a COUNT even though auth_required is false.

Link The question Asked as A broken answer
1 · relayList Do we know where you post? kind 10002, one fetch no list, or nothing on wss://
2 · scoreList Do you name a service whose scores rank? kind 10040, one fetch absent, or no 30382:rank with a relay hint
3 · scores Have that service's cards arrived here? COUNT kind 30382, here and there 0 here — the relay answered, and holds none
4 · ranked Does a ranked read actually come back? two NIP-50 searches, authed and anonymous, both since = 24h anon returns rows, authed returns none

The trap this replaced

The ranked probe's since is not tidiness. Measured against search-staging: that search returns 12 events immediately with a 24-hour since, and times out with none at all without one. Both sides then come back zero, which the chain correctly reads as a quiet window rather than a broken lens — so link 4 passes every time while testing nothing. It shipped that way once.

06 · The storage chain

Asked at pre-flight, on purpose

The second chain answers a different question — can you host your paper? — and fails independently of the first. It used to be checked at publish time, which is after an edition has been generated and paid for: the reader learned they had nowhere to put their paper at the one moment the answer was most expensive. It is now asked before anything is written, and it still never blocks the model call.

07 · Past the gate, still before the call

What the corpus has to survive

  1. Pull the corpus — one REQ per desk

    Every desk asks on its own subscription, all at once, alongside the anonymous control run and an uncapped COUNT of the day's notes. Sharing one REQ meant recovering results by kind, which works right up until two filters share one — and two do: the control run is kind 1 exactly like the notes desk. Merged, its anonymous results were filed as news.

    Every ranked filter carries observer:<pk> sort:rank filter:rank:gte:20. The floor is not redundant with the cap: it turns limit=400 from a target back into a ceiling, so a thin lens or a quiet day stops scraping downward for material nobody vouched for.

  2. Refuse a window with nothing in it

    corpus.notes.isEmpty() throws Refused(QUIET). A thin paper is the right response to a quiet day; zero notes is not a thin paper, it is no paper. This is the second and last thing that can stop a run before the model is called.

  3. Read the overlap — an alarm, not a gate

    How many events the lens surfaced that the same window without it also returned. Near zero is the product thesis stated as a number. Near 100% is the desk-collision bug above, announcing itself in the progress list rather than in an edition three weeks later. Nothing stops on it; it is an instrument.

  4. Shortlist the art

    Candidates are chosen and hotlinked — never fetched, resized, re-hosted or inlined. There is no image library in this project, so nothing here can fail on somebody else's server.

  5. Fit the digest to the budget

    250,000 characters, about 62,500 tokens, split as an even share per desk rather than spent in desk order — otherwise the notes desk ate the budget and the quiet desks below it printed nothing. What is kept and what is pruned is reported before the call, not after.

  6. Only now: Step.Writing

    One streaming call, a fixed system prompt that never reaches the client, and the house stylesheet. Roughly a dollar fifty, and every check above exists to make sure it is a dollar fifty spent on a paper somebody can actually be shown.

quartz owns NIP-19 decoding but not this guard, and the difference matters: decodePublicKeyAsHexOrNull decodes any 32-byte bech32 payload, so an nsec turns into the hex of the secret key rather than returning null. Without this check, a reader who pastes their private key into the front door would have it put into a relay filter and sent over the wire.

09 · The other side of the call

What deliberately is not checked here

Three checks run after the model call, and belong there. The model writes the whole document, markup and stylesheet; safety is enforced after generation by the sanitizer, not before it by constraining the writer.

The sanitizer proves the page cannot misbehave — no <a href> to the open web survives, only permalinks back to a source event. The proof opens it in a browser and, if it does not read, regenerates once and then drops the author's stylesheet for the house one. The validator proves it does not lie, and it is the only gate left before an edition reaches the reader's own archive: a page that quotes something it cannot find in a source event is not published, though the reader who paid for it still gets to read it.

That division is the whole shape of the thing. Everything on this page is about whether the call is worth making. Everything after it is about whether the answer is worth keeping.
